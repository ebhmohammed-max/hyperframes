# EP17 "1926: ஒரு சாதாரண நாள்": fact sheet

Written 20 Nov 2026 from web search extracts only (no full page was read, no book was opened). Lines marked **check** rest on one source, on a date that does not match 1926 exactly, or on memory, and should be confirmed before the film is published. Tags in the script: S census or official number, H historical record, R memoir or recollection, O historians' view.

The family, the house and every scene are invented; the film says so in its last chapter (N07). Only the numbers and events below are claimed as fact.

## Setting
- "100 years ago" is taken as 1926; the place is Madras city (today Chennai), capital of the Madras Presidency under British rule.
- Madras city population: 526,911 (census 1921), 647,232 (census 1931). Script: "about 5,27,000". Source: Wikipedia, Demographics of Chennai.
- Chennai metropolitan area today "over 1 crore": **check** (the 2011 census gives 86.5 lakh for the urban agglomeration; later estimates pass 1 crore).
- Madras Presidency population 1921: 42,365,735 (not spoken).
- The Presidency covered today's Tamil Nadu, most of Andhra, Malabar (north Kerala), South Canara and Bellary (Karnataka) and Ganjam (Odisha). The script names four present-day states' parts. The map outline in the film is drawn by hand and labelled approximate.
- Coins carried George V (King-Emperor 1910–36). Independence came in 1947, 21 years later.
- 1 rupee = 16 annas; 1 anna = 12 pies; 1 rupee = 192 pies.

## Morning (N02)
- Street lights: 6,300 kerosene lamps in 1911; all replaced by 8,300 electric lights in 1924 (The Hindu, Madras Day feature). Script: "two years before", "about 8,300".
- Electricity inside houses limited to a few bungalows: **check** (general statement, no number found).
- Water from wells and street taps; cooking on firewood; grinding stones: general knowledge, no source needed beyond common histories.
- Coffee as a new habit: A.R. Venkatachalapathy, "In those days there was no coffee" (IESHR 39, 2002): morning coffee replaced rice water (நீராகாரம்) among Tamils in the early 1900s and was a way of life by the 1930s. Tag O.

## Travel and money (N03)
- Electric trams in Madras since 1895: **check** the year against a second source (widely given as 7 May 1895, the first electric tramway in India).
- Tram cars: 88 in 1925–26, 89 in 1926–27; 15,510,007 passengers in 1925–26 (IJRSS, Feb 2020). Script: "about 90 cars", "over 1.5 crore trips".
- Fare "within one anna": recollections give ¾ anna to 1 anna; the original fare cap was ½ anna a mile in second class (The Hindu; The Federal). Undated memories, tag R: **check**.
- Jutkas, hand-pulled rickshaws, bicycles on the roads: The Federal; DT Next.
- Wages: unskilled mill worker 12 annas 6 pies a day; farm labour near Madras 6 to 8 annas (figures for about 1918–21, not 1926): **check**.
- Rice "about 4 padi a rupee": **check** (4 measures a rupee was a 1920 demand, not a recorded 1926 price).
- Buses: the tram company ran the first organised bus service 1925–28, starting with 12 buses. Script only says cars and buses had "just begun to appear".

## School and women (N04)
- Literacy, census 1921: Madras Presidency 8.57% (male 15.22%); all India 7.2% (male 12.2, female 1.8). Script: "fewer than 10 in 100"; women "two or three in 100": the Presidency female figure itself was not found, **check**.
- Slate, veranda (திண்ணை) schools, one or two years of schooling: general statement, **check**.
- No minimum marriage age in law in 1926. Child Marriage Restraint Act (Sarda Act) passed 1929: 14 for girls, 18 for boys. Script: "three years later". The boys' age is from memory: **check**.
- Dr Muthulakshmi Reddy nominated to the Madras Legislative Council in 1926, the first woman legislator in British India (some sources date her taking the seat to 1927).

## News and distance (N05)
- Swadesamitran: Tamil daily (founded 1882, daily from 1899: from memory, **check**).
- Ananda Vikatan began in February 1926 as a monthly; S.S. Vasan bought it in 1928 (Wikipedia).
- Post card reply "up to a week"; telegrams charged by the word: general statements, **check**.
- Radio: Madras Presidency Radio Club, first broadcast 31 July 1924 from Egmore, 40-watt transmitter, range about 5 miles, closed October 1927 for lack of money (Madras Musings).
- Boat Mail: Madras Egmore to Dhanushkodi pier from 24 February 1914, about 675 km; steamer to Talaimannar; train on to Colombo. "About 20 hours" to Dhanushkodi comes from 1950s timings: **check** for the 1920s. "More than a day and a half" in all is my sum: **check**.
- Madras to Colombo by air today about 1½ hours: **check**.
- Dhanushkodi destroyed by a cyclone in December 1964 (38 years after 1926).
- The rail route on the map is a rough sketch through hand-set points and is labelled so.

## Entertainment (N06)
- Permanent cinemas in Madras since the 1910s (Gaiety, Mount Road, 1912). Silent films with title cards in Tamil and English.
- A narrator beside the screen and live harmonium and tabla: described in histories of Tamil silent cinema, tag O: **check**.
- Kalidas, the first Tamil talkie, 31 October 1931.
- All-night stage plays without microphones; gramophones in well-off homes: general statements.
- John Logie Baird's first public demonstration of television, London, 26 January 1926: from memory, **check**.
- Television in Madras: Doordarshan station opened 15 August 1975: from memory, **check**. Script: "about 50 years".

## Night and the number 27 (N01, N07)
- Life expectancy at birth, India 1921–31: 26.8 years (male 26.9, female 26.6) (MOSPI compilation).
- Infant mortality, India 1921–31: about 241 per 1,000 live births (Malaker and Guha Roy, Sankhya 1990). Script: "about 240", "one in four".
- "Many who got past childhood lived past 50 or 60": a fair reading of how such averages work, tag O; no table was checked: **check**.
- No antibiotics in 1926. Penicillin discovered 1928 (Fleming); in general use from the 1940s: from memory, **check**.
- Cholera, smallpox, plague, malaria common in the 1920s: general statement.
- 1918 influenza: over 1 crore deaths in India (estimates run from 1.2 to 1.8 crore): from memory, **check**.
