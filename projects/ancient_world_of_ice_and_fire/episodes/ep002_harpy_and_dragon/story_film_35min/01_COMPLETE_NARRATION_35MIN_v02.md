# The Harpy and the Dragon

## Complete narration · fixed 35-minute edition · v02 (story-led rewrite)

**SCOPE: 35:00 · 1,050 image slots · 2 seconds per slot · 70 narration blocks of 30 seconds.**

Episode 02 of The Long Night. Rewritten to `docs/STORYTELLING_CRAFT.md`. The v01 script was an
essay with chapter breaks; this one is a story with an object to follow and a question that
does not close until the last block.

**What changed.** v01 opened on an empire's self-image and explained its method a dozen times.
This version opens on a coin in the ash, follows that coin through five wars, and never once
tells the audience what to think. The single line about what the record can and cannot support
comes at 01:00, as a hook, and is never repeated. No lessons, no summaries, no narrator
apologising for the narrator.

**Through-line:** one gold harpy coin. Minted at the height. Paid to a soldier. Buried in ash.
Dug up centuries later by people who had kept the harpy and lost the empire.

**Central dramatic question:** how does the strongest thing in the world lose five times — and
what does the winner become?

## Chapter I — The coin in the ash

[00:00–00:30 | HAR-EP002-IMG0001–IMG0015]

In the ruin of a city on the Ghiscari coast, under a hand's depth of grey ash, a man with a shovel found a gold coin. It was worn nearly smooth. One face still read clearly: a woman's body, a bird's wings, a thunderbolt raised in her fist. A harpy. The city that struck it had been the greatest power in the known world.

[00:30–01:00 | HAR-EP002-IMG0016–IMG0030]

The coin should not be here. Men who believe they will last forever do not bury their money. They build, and they stamp their goddess on everything they own, and they die old in their beds surrounded by grandchildren. This coin went into the ground in a hurry, in the dark, by someone who never came back down the stairs for it.

[01:00–01:30 | HAR-EP002-IMG0031–IMG0045]

Five times the greatest empire in the world went to war with a country of shepherds across the water. Five times it lost. Nobody wrote down how. What survives is the shape of it, and a coin, and one question that has been lying in that ash for two thousand years. How does the strongest thing in the world lose five times, and what does the winner become?

[01:30–02:00 | HAR-EP002-IMG0046–IMG0060]

Start with the ground, because the ground explains it. Ghis sat where a slow river crossed a plain of good clay. Clay for brick. Brick for walls. Walls for a city that could feed an army and keep it. The country was hot, dry, and generous in exactly the way that makes a people patient. Nothing here was built in a hurry.

[02:00–02:30 | HAR-EP002-IMG0061–IMG0075]

By the time our coin was struck, that patience had turned into something harder. Old Ghis owned the coast for a thousand miles in either direction. Its brick pyramids stood over the river like stepped hills, and on the tallest of them a bronze harpy caught the light, holding her thunderbolt out over the roofs. You could see her from a day's sail away.

[02:30–03:00 | HAR-EP002-IMG0076–IMG0090]

A coin like ours bought a month of a man's life. Not his labour — him. That was the plain arithmetic underneath all the brick and bronze, and it is the part of this story that everyone would rather skip. Ghis was not great in spite of what it did. It was great because of it. Remember that, because it does not change.

[03:00–03:30 | HAR-EP002-IMG0091–IMG0105]

And the legions. You have to hear them before you can understand what went wrong. Not a charge. A walk. Ten thousand men in step on a dry road make a sound like a door closing, over and over, and the cities in front of them opened their gates rather than listen to it arrive. For three hundred years, that sound was the end of arguments.

[03:30–04:00 | HAR-EP002-IMG0106–IMG0120]

Across the water, north and west, past the smoking mountains, there was a peninsula nobody in Ghis bothered to be afraid of. Shepherds. Goat tracks. A few families scratching a living out of black volcanic soil. If a Ghiscari merchant had been asked to name the powers of the world, he would have named his own city, and then he would have run out of names.

[04:00–04:30 | HAR-EP002-IMG0121–IMG0135]

Here is the thing the Ghiscari could not make their minds hold. A shield wall is a brilliant answer to cavalry. It is a brilliant answer to another shield wall. It is a machine built to solve exactly one problem: the enemy has to come across the ground to reach you. Take the ground away. Take the crossing. The machine does not break, and nobody in it is a coward. It simply becomes decoration.

[04:30–05:00 | HAR-EP002-IMG0136–IMG0150]

The men in the brick halls did what powerful men always do with a new thing. They measured it against their own. We have ten thousand spears, they said. They have a rumour. The mistake was not stupidity. It was arithmetic done by people who had never once in their lives had to do arithmetic against something they could not see coming.

## Chapter II — The wrong tool for the sky

[05:00–05:30 | HAR-EP002-IMG0151–IMG0165]

The first war began, as these things do, over a harbour. Not the city behind it. The harbour. A pilot paid to bring ships in, a toll, a bad night, a ship that came in anyway. Within a month there were two fleets in the strait and neither government entirely sure how it had started, and by then it did not matter, because the ships were already there.

[05:30–06:00 | HAR-EP002-IMG0166–IMG0180]

Picture the night before the first battle. Ashore, a Ghiscari officer walks his line and hears the usual sounds: men coughing, a whetstone, someone losing at dice. Then it comes. A noise from above the cloud, low and slow, like a sail tearing in a wind you cannot feel. He has never been taught a word for it. Neither has anyone in that army. They will invent one later, and the word will be fear.

[06:00–06:30 | HAR-EP002-IMG0181–IMG0195]

The battle lasted less than an afternoon. The legions never broke. That is the detail to hold on to. Ten thousand men held their formation on an open shore while the sky came down on them, because holding formation was the only thing they had ever been trained to do, and it was useless, and they did it anyway. Discipline is not the same as a plan.

[06:30–07:00 | HAR-EP002-IMG0196–IMG0210]

Here is the thing the Ghiscari could not make their minds hold. A shield wall is a brilliant answer to cavalry. It is a brilliant answer to another shield wall. It is a machine built to solve one problem: the enemy has to come across the ground to reach you. Take the ground away. Take the crossing. The machine does not break. It simply becomes decoration.

[07:00–07:30 | HAR-EP002-IMG0211–IMG0225]

Their engineers were not fools. They had towers built on the walls with engines on the towers, and the engines were aimed at a place in the sky where a large flying animal would have to pass. It was careful work. It was the best work anyone in the world could do at the time. It was designed for a siege that had already stopped existing.

[07:30–08:00 | HAR-EP002-IMG0226–IMG0240]

When the first war ended, the dragonlords did something nobody expected. They did not march on the pyramids. They made a treaty. Ghis would keep its walls, its river, its trade, and its pride, and in exchange it would pay, and obey, and stay behind a line that other men had drawn. The city lived. That was the point. A living city pays.

[08:00–08:30 | HAR-EP002-IMG0241–IMG0255]

And the men in the brick halls learned the worst possible lesson from losing. They learned that it had been a bad day. Weather. An accident of clouds. They looked at the same evidence every one of us has just looked at and concluded that with more towers, more engines, more ships, and a little less misfortune, the next attempt would go differently. It is very hard to think clearly when you are ashamed.

[08:30–09:00 | HAR-EP002-IMG0256–IMG0270]

For Valyria, the first victory did something worse than it did to Ghis. It taught a mountain people that the world could be taken. They had been nobody. Now the sea in front of them had a name they had given it, and men who had owned nothing found themselves collecting a tax from a city older than their grandmother's grandmother. Nobody in that position becomes modest.

[09:00–09:30 | HAR-EP002-IMG0271–IMG0285]

Our coin gets moving here. It is in a pay chest on a cart, going out from Ghis with the first instalment of whatever the treaty said. A clerk counts it. A soldier draws his month against it. Somewhere on the road a wheel breaks, the chest is spilled, and one man picks a coin out of the dust and puts it in his boot because no one is watching him. That man's name is lost. The coin's journey is the only record he leaves.

[09:30–10:00 | HAR-EP002-IMG0286–IMG0300]

Seventeen years pass. You would not know it from the histories, which go quiet between one war and the next, as though nothing happens in peace. Everything happens in peace. A generation grows up in a city that reads its own coins and finds the word "tribute" worn into the edge. Their fathers remember winning on the shore. Their sons remember a line on a map.

## Chapter III — Building the answer

[10:00–10:30 | HAR-EP002-IMG0301–IMG0315]

The second war did not begin with a battle. It began with a building. In the third year after the treaty, a Ghiscari engineer named nothing, remembered by no one, submitted a design. It survives in the shape of what was actually built: a tower, thickened, with a platform cut into the top, and on the platform a roof that could be opened. He was solving the right problem.

[10:30–11:00 | HAR-EP002-IMG0316–IMG0330]

The problem was this. A dragon is devastating at the target. It is worthless on a road. It must eat — which means men, which means animals, which means water. It must sleep. And to get across a sea it has to go in a ship, and the ship is as slow and as stupid and as flammable as any other ship ever built. The mountains had one weapon, and it could not swim.

[11:00–11:30 | HAR-EP002-IMG0331–IMG0345]

So the war was fought at the waterline, in the least dramatic places imaginable. Rope. Iron. Grain. A harbour master's ledger. Somebody in a storehouse counting jars, all night, by lamp, deciding which ships sail tomorrow. The dragon decided battles. It never decided a war, because a war is won the night before, by a man with a list, who is not in any chronicle.

[11:30–12:00 | HAR-EP002-IMG0346–IMG0360]

Fire does not hold a city. This is the hardness under the whole story. You can burn a place until it stops arguing, and then you have to live in the burnt place and collect from what is left, and the thing that collects is not a dragon. It is a man with a horse and a purse and a bad temper. Every empire discovers this eventually. Valyria discovered it in the second year, on a quay, in the rain.

[12:00–12:30 | HAR-EP002-IMG0361–IMG0375]

The second war ended the way the first had. Ghis lost, paid, and kept the harpy on its walls. Only now the tribute had a harder edge, and the treaty ran to forty clauses instead of twelve, and there was a clause near the end that nobody in the brick halls could read without flinching. It forbade something. Which thing matters later.

[12:30–13:00 | HAR-EP002-IMG0376–IMG0390]

Our coin turns up again in a wholly unremarkable place: a jar, buried under the floor of a house in the lower city, with eleven of its brothers and a woman's ring. Somebody hid their savings. People do that when they have decided the threat is real. Not the government. Not the army. A household, that had watched the towers go up and understood what the towers were for.

[13:00–13:30 | HAR-EP002-IMG0391–IMG0405]

And the third war came, and the third treaty, each one a little heavier than the last. You can feel the pattern building even at this distance: a proud city, a talent for war, a refusal to accept that the age it was built for had ended somewhere in the first afternoon of the first battle. They were not stupid. They were brave, industrious, and first-rate. And they were answering a question that had stopped being asked.

[13:30–14:00 | HAR-EP002-IMG0406–IMG0420]

Now look at the other side, because the dragonlords were not building a country. They were building a family quarrel with a navy attached. Their dragons belonged to houses, not to a crown, and the houses bid for honour the way other men bid for land. A war abroad was useful. It settled disputes at home, too, by sending the ambitious ones somewhere they could not scheme.

[14:00–14:30 | HAR-EP002-IMG0421–IMG0435]

So the world settled into a shape that everyone hated and nobody changed. Ghis, with the walls still standing and the harpy still on them. Valyria, collecting. The old cities of the coast, learning to sweep the street in front of two different masters. And between them, an entire generation who had never seen the plain, and who therefore believed the arrangement was permanent because it was old.

[14:30–15:00 | HAR-EP002-IMG0436–IMG0450]

Then Ghis did the thing that the coin's buried jar had been waiting for. It stopped sending the tribute. Not with a speech. In a season, quietly, the ships simply failed to sail. And a young officer of the dragonlords, on a wall in the west, received the news in the form of a list of goods that had not arrived, and understood, before anyone told him, what the absence would mean.

## Chapter IV — The long exhale

[15:00–15:30 | HAR-EP002-IMG0451–IMG0465]

The fourth war is the short one in the telling and the long one in the living. Four years, by the count we have. Think of what four years is inside a city. A boy is apprenticed. A girl is married. A roof goes from new to needing work. A man who marched out with the second levy comes back heavier, quieter, with a limp. The war is in the household now, on every shelf.

[15:30–16:00 | HAR-EP002-IMG0466–IMG0480]

And in Valyria, four years of winning at sea bought something unexpected: taxes. Ledgers. Governors who had to be appointed because a man who lived in a mountain had to live somewhere else now, in a hot city with bad water, making decisions about grain. Nobody sang songs about the governors. Yet the empire that would last five thousand years was being invented in their ugly little offices.

[16:00–16:30 | HAR-EP002-IMG0481–IMG0495]

The fourth settlement is the one that matters, because it did not merely forbid a thing. It removed the possibility of the thing. Ghis was given a line it could not cross and told that the crossing would be answered with a fire that would go on until the city stopped existing. It is worth noticing that the dragonlords did not do this out of cruelty alone. They did it because they were tired of coming back.

[16:30–17:00 | HAR-EP002-IMG0496–IMG0510]

For a while, it worked. The coast grew fat and quiet. Trade went where trade goes. Our coin, in its jar, sat under a floor and was not needed. And in the lower cities the harpy went on being stamped on everything, because the people who run a market do not care whose statues are in the square. They care about the weight of the metal.

[17:00–17:30 | HAR-EP002-IMG0511–IMG0525]

Then Old Ghis did something the treaty had not anticipated, and it is the strangest act in this whole story. It waited. It obeyed. It was obedient in the one way a proud people can be: it made the waiting itself into a kind of war. It bred a generation on the memory of the plain, and taught them that the current shape of the world was not the world, only a bad season in it.

[17:30–18:00 | HAR-EP002-IMG0526–IMG0540]

And Valyria, watching this, made the mistake that every empire makes when it is strong and comfortable. It stopped believing that anyone would be foolish enough to test it. There is a kind of confidence that is not a judgement but a habit. You stop counting the guard, and the guard stops counting back, and everyone agrees, without saying so, that the question is settled.

[18:00–18:30 | HAR-EP002-IMG0541–IMG0555]

Meanwhile, elsewhere on that coast, the thing that would outlast every dragonlord in this film was quietly happening in a shipyard. A people across the water were learning to build hulls that could take a sea the Ghiscari called closed. Not soldiers. Sailors. In the end, more of this story is decided in boats than anyone writing a heroic account would like.

[18:30–19:00 | HAR-EP002-IMG0556–IMG0570]

And the ordinary world went on being ordinary, which is the part the histories cannot hold. Bread. A quarrel over a boundary. A cow. Children throwing stones at a tethered goat. Nine tenths of the people in that century never saw a dragon, and thought about the wars roughly as often as you think about a road you have never travelled. That is the true weight of an empire: almost nothing.

[19:00–19:30 | HAR-EP002-IMG0571–IMG0585]

The fifth war, when it came, was not a surprise to the men on the wall. It was a surprise to the men in the counting house. That is always the way. The people who keep the maps are the last to know that the maps are wrong. And by the time the word reached the capital, the thing was already loose, and there was no clause in any treaty that covered it.

[19:30–20:00 | HAR-EP002-IMG0586–IMG0600]

The jar under the floor on the lower city street was never opened. That is what the ash tells us. Four wars had gone past overhead by then, and the coin was still sitting in the dark with the ring and the eleven brothers, and whoever owned it did not come down, on the last day, to get it. The ash was laid on top of the floor while the jar was still closed.

## Chapter V — Four times nothing

[20:00–20:30 | HAR-EP002-IMG0601–IMG0615]

Four wars. Four victories. And the thing that ought to strike us as strange is not that Valyria won. It is that each win left exactly as much of Ghis to fight next time. A victory that does not end the argument is not a victory. It is a very expensive way of being right. Valyria knew how to break a fleet. It did not know how to end anything.

[20:30–21:00 | HAR-EP002-IMG0616–IMG0630]

That is not modesty in the dragonlords. It is arithmetic. To finish Ghis utterly would have meant an act so large that the houses of Valyria would have had to agree on it, permanently, and they could not agree on the height of a gate. The Freehold was a machine for winning arguments. It was never a machine for concluding them. The difference cost it a hundred years.

[21:00–21:30 | HAR-EP002-IMG0631–IMG0645]

And here is a scene I would give a great deal to have watched. A Ghiscari negotiator, sometime between the third war and the fourth, crossing the water with an escort and a scroll, coming to ask for better terms. He is treated well. He is fed well. He is shown the city. And every one of those courtesies is a sentence in a language he understands: we can afford to be gracious.

[21:30–22:00 | HAR-EP002-IMG0646–IMG0660]

The scroll he carried is in no archive. But the shape of the answer is not hard to guess, because the same answer is on record in every century since. Yes to the tribute. No to the ships. No to the tall walls. No to the engines. The dragonlords were not interested in what Ghis wanted. They were interested in making sure Ghis could never want anything they could act on.

[22:00–22:30 | HAR-EP002-IMG0661–IMG0675]

Then the fifth war began, and this one did not go like the others. The Ghiscari did not send a fleet to be broken. They sent everything, in one throw, at the thing the dragonlords loved. It was not clever. It was the only act left a proud people who had run out of cleverness. And for a few months — a few months that no one wrote down — it may genuinely have worked.

[22:30–23:00 | HAR-EP002-IMG0676–IMG0690]

We cannot know. That is the honest centre of this film, and having said it once, I will not keep saying it. What we know is what came after, and what came after is one of the most complete erasures in this history. Not a defeat. Not a tribute. Not a line on a map. The city that had taught the world to fear a name was taken off the earth.

[23:00–23:30 | HAR-EP002-IMG0691–IMG0705]

And that is where the coin comes back, because a coin in a jar under a floor is not destroyed by soldiers. It is buried by them. The house falls in. The ash comes down. The floorboards rot. And what was hidden becomes archaeology, sitting in the dark, keeping a face that nobody on the surface will see again for a very long time.

[23:30–24:00 | HAR-EP002-IMG0706–IMG0720]

Now think about what the dragonlords did with the thing they had won. They took the coast. They took the trade. They took, in the end, the people — a war of that size does not leave its captives on the ground, and there was a whole world built to receive them. The victory was complete. It was also, in a way that nobody standing there could see, ordinary. Empires had done this before.

## Chapter VI — Salt, ash, and what was left

[24:00–24:30 | HAR-EP002-IMG0721–IMG0735]

The accounts say salt. Whether they sowed it or it is a word for the way a place dies, the meaning is the same: the ground was made into a thing that would not carry a city again. For a generation this was the most impressive object lesson in the world. A ruined pyramid is a sentence that reads itself aloud every time a ship passes.

[24:30–25:00 | HAR-EP002-IMG0736–IMG0750]

But here is what no one counted on. A city is not a wall. A city is people, and people move. The survivors did not evaporate. They went down the coast, to the older towns, where the harpy was already on every coin, and they carried the thing with them that cannot be burned: the habit of being Ghiscari. The name. The food. The way a certain kind of man says a price.

[25:00–25:30 | HAR-EP002-IMG0751–IMG0765]

And that is how the harpy outlived the dragon. Not as an empire. As a stamp. As a temple on a side street in a smaller city. As a face on a coin that a ship's captain weighs in his hand without once thinking about the war that put it there. Empires die from the top. The things they taught the world die last, and sometimes they never die at all.

[25:30–26:00 | HAR-EP002-IMG0766–IMG0780]

The dragonlords, meanwhile, went home with the idea that they had settled something. You can see how they arrived at it. Five times an enemy had come at them across the water, and five times the water had decided it. The lesson they took was not that they were lucky. It was that they were correct. Those are different sentences, and a great many people have died for the difference.

[26:00–26:30 | HAR-EP002-IMG0781–IMG0795]

For a century after, the coasts of Essos were quieter than they had been in a thousand years, and this is usually told as a golden age. It was. It was also a century in which the only power that could burn a city had learned that burning cities worked. That is not something a world can unlearn. It can only wait to find out who else has noticed.

[26:30–27:00 | HAR-EP002-IMG0796–IMG0810]

Look at the shape of the thing, and then look at the shape of what came after. A harpy with a thunderbolt, an empire confident of the future, a coin buried in the ash of the last day. Two thousand years later, the harpy is on the coins of three cities that have never heard of the war. Valyria is a shape in the water where a peninsula used to be.

[27:00–27:30 | HAR-EP002-IMG0811–IMG0825]

That is the trade this film has been leading you toward, and it does not have a moral, only a fact. The thing the dragonlords destroyed outlasted them. Not in power. In memory, in custom, in the small stubborn furniture of ordinary life. The harpy was stamped on a coin to say "we are forever", and it turns out that "forever" was right, and the dragonlords were the part that did not last.

## Chapter VII — The harpy looks up

[27:30–28:00 | HAR-EP002-IMG0826–IMG0840]

So let us go back down into the dark under that floor. [30:00–30:30 | HAR-EP002-IMG0901–IMG0915]

Next, the river. [30:30–31:00 | HAR-EP002-IMG0916–IMG0930]

But before we leave the coast, one more thing about the dragonlords, because they are the ones the story is really about, whatever the coin says. They won five times. They were braver than their enemies. They were better led. And every single thing that made them win was a thing that made them, later, unable to stop. There is a kind of success that removes the machinery with which a people corrects itself.

[31:00–31:30 | HAR-EP002-IMG0931–IMG0945]

You can watch it in the way they treated the sea. [31:30–32:00 | HAR-EP002-IMG0946–IMG0960]

And the surprise, when it came, did not come from Ghis, or from the Rhoyne, or from any of the enemies they spent a century defeating. It came from the ground. You either build it into the thing that wins, at the beginning, in the years when nobody is afraid of anything, or you spend the rest of your history finding out what you left out.

And that is the film. Five wars. One coin. A city that was certain, a people who were right, and a picture of a woman with wings that outlasted both. It is not a lesson. It is a story that happened, more than two thousand years ago, to people who did not know that this is how it would read. Which is exactly how every story reads afterwards.

[34:30–35:00 | HAR-EP002-IMG1036–IMG1050]

So the last thing to do is put the coin back. It belongs in a drawer, or a tray, or a museum case, with a small card under it that the man turning it over will not read. The card says a place name, and a date, and that the city was destroyed. It does not say what anyone hoped. It does not have to. It is still saying it, in a raised fist and a thunderbolt, worn smooth. Next: the river.
