# Episode 02 picture: the honest arithmetic

The film is built on a 70-block grid. Each block is one **scene**, 30 seconds long,
carrying 15 plate slots of 2 seconds each. That grid is what generates 1,050 slots —
but it is not 1,050 separate pictures. It is **70 scenes**, each photographed fifteen
ways.

This distinction is the whole cost question, so it should be exact.

## What the episodes actually need

|                      | Episode 01                         | Episode 02        |
| -------------------- | ---------------------------------- | ----------------- |
| Blocks (scenes)      | 70                                 | 70                |
| Plate slots          | 1,050                              | 1,050             |
| Unique scene masters | 70                                 | 70 (not yet made) |
| Plates per scene     | 15                                 | 15                |
| Image source         | produced over 105 batch deliveries | none              |

Episode 01 was assembled the expensive way: every one of its 1,050 plates is a distinct
picture. That is why it took so long, and it is why its material cannot simply be
re-used without Episode 02 looking like Episode 01.

Episode 02 does not have to repeat that. A documentary scene is shot from different
angles, not drawn from fifteen unrelated pictures. One master per scene, cropped and
reframed fifteen ways, is the standard grammar — and at 1376×768 with a 1.06–1.10 push
already applied to every plate, the motion hides the cropping.

## The cost, stated plainly

- **70 generated masters** = 7 turns of generation at 10 per turn.
- **1,050 unique plates** = 105 turns, which is not a plan.

So the realistic route is 70 masters. What that buys and what it costs:

**Buys:** a film that looks like Episode 02. Correct subjects — harpy statues, Ghiscari
brick, Valyrian steel, the burning plain. Fifteen framings per scene means the eye never
sees an obvious repeat, because the camera is genuinely moving within a scene rather
than cutting between strangers.

**Costs:** it breaks the literal contract clause "1,050 unique images". If you want that
clause honoured, say so and I will say plainly that it is 105 turns and roughly a week
of uninterrupted work, and you can decide whether that is worth it.

## What is true right now

- 1,050 plate slots exist and are filled with Episode 01's images.
- Those images are the _wrong episode_: cool sea, dragon silhouettes, Valyrian coast.
  Episode 02 is ochre desert, brick ziggurats, bronze harpies. The two do not mix.
- Everything else about the episode is finished and correct: script, voice, mix, timing,
  cards, place names, effects. **Replacing plates moves none of it** — the builder derives
  every card and effect from measured audio timings, not from the pictures.

That last point is the reason this is a solvable problem rather than a rebuild.
