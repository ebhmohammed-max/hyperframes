THE HARPY AND THE DRAGON — How the Strongest Empire in the World Lost Five Times
The Long Night | Episode 02

How does the strongest thing in the world lose five times — and what does the winner become?

A gold harpy coin, worn nearly smooth, dug out of the ash of a city that had been the greatest power in the known world. This is the five wars of Old Ghis against Valyria, told through that coin: minted at the height of an empire, paid out as tribute, dropped in the dust of a road, buried in a jar with eleven of its brothers and a woman's ring, and never dug up again by the people who hid it.

The legions never broke, and it did not matter. The dragons decided the battles and never decided the war. Four times the strongest city in the world was beaten — and four times it was left with exactly as much of itself as it would need to try again.

The accounts of the fifth war are thin, and this film says so once rather than pretending otherwise. What survives is the ground, the shape of the thing, and a picture of a woman with wings that outlived both of the empires that fought over it.
