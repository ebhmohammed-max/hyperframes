# EP20 fact sheet: 80 டன் கல்லு 200 அடி உயரத்துக்கு எப்படி போச்சு?

Script v1, 23 Nov 2026. Each line of the film carries one of these labels on screen:
**கல்வெட்டு பதிவு** (K, an inscription records it) · **ஆய்வாளர் கூற்று** (A, a scholar or a study says it) · **மரபுக் கதை** (M, the legend as it is told) · **நம்ம கணக்கு** (N, our own sum, assumptions below) · **பதிவான உண்மை** (F) · **கருத்து** (O, our opinion or inference).

**The one-line finding.** The legend says one 80-ton stone went up a ramp four miles long. Architectural historians say the cap is built of several pieces; the inscriptions, which record almost everything else, record nothing about how the temple was built; and a ramp of that length would have needed about two hundred times more earth than the temple has stone. How the top was actually raised is not recorded anywhere, and the film says so.

## 4. Our own sums (label N)

These are rough sums made for this film to test the legend. They are not from any study and are labelled "நம்ம கணக்கு" on screen.

**The slope.** 60 m of height over 6,400 m of length is 1 in 107: "walk a hundred feet, rise one foot".

**Pulling the stone.** On that slope the pull needed is the weight × (slope + rolling resistance). With log rollers on packed earth the resistance is taken as 5 to 10 % of the weight: 80,000 kg × (0.01 + 0.07) ≈ 6,400 kg of pull. A man pulls 25 to 30 kg steadily, so 200 to 260 men: "a few hundred people". A dozen or so elephants could do the same.

**The earth in the ramp.** A bank of loose earth stands only with sloping sides. Take a road 8 m wide on top and sides that go out 1.5 m for every metre of height (1 m out for well-rammed earth). The cross-section at height h is 8h + 1.5h²; summed along a ramp rising evenly from 0 to 60 m over 6,400 m this gives 1.5 + 11.5 ≈ **13 million m³** (9 million with the steeper sides). The film says "about one crore (10 million) cubic metres".

**Against the temple.** 10,000,000 ÷ 50,000 = **200 times** the stone of the whole temple (180 to 260 times across the two assumptions).

**A ten-ton piece on a short ramp.** Slope 1 in 10: 10,000 kg × (0.10 + 0.07) = 1,700 kg of pull; at 30 kg a man, 57 men: "fifty or sixty people".

**Stone per day.** 130,000 tons ÷ (7 × 365 = 2,555 days) = **51 tons a day** on average, every day for seven years. The film says "on average 50 tons a day" and "2,500 days".

## 5. What is opinion (label O)

- That the inscriptions are silent about the top stone *because* it went up in pieces. It is an inference of ours. Chola inscriptions record gifts and duties, not building work, so the silence proves nothing by itself.
- That rollers, ropes, levers and elephants "were enough".
- That the real wonder is the daily flow of stone, not the one block.

## To check before publishing

1. **The pieces.** The strongest sources (Branfoot; The Hindu 2010) say "several pieces". Nobody we could find prints the number of pieces or a drawing. If a viewer asks "how many?", the honest answer is "not published".
2. **80 tons against 25 tons.** These may be two different blocks (the square slab and the dome). The script does not say that the dome weighs 25 tons instead of 80; keep it that way in the title and thumbnail as well.
3. **216 ft against 196 ft.** Both figures are in circulation; the ASI still gives 216 ft.
4. **64 inscriptions.** From secondary pages.
5. **Our sums** are school arithmetic with stated assumptions, not an engineering study. Say so in the description.
6. **Respect.** The film does not say the temple is less of a wonder, and does not blame anyone for the legend. The thumbnail and title should not read as "the big temple story is a lie".
7. Every picture is an AI illustration. None shows the real temple exactly. Say so in the description.
