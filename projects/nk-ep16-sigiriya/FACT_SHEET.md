# EP16 Sigiriya: fact sheet

Written 19 Nov 2026 from web search extracts only (no full page was read, no book was opened). Lines marked **check** rest on one source, on sources that disagree, or on memory, and should be confirmed before the film is published. Tags used in the script: H record, M the Culavamsa chronicle alone, L belief, O scholars' opinion, C unproven claim.

## The site (H)
- Sigiriya, Matale District, Central Province, near Dambulla. Name from Sinhagiri, "lion rock".
- The rock stands about 180 m above the plain (often rounded to "nearly 200 m"), about 350 to 360 m above sea level. Summit about 1.5 to 1.6 hectares. Script says 180 m and "about 1.5 hectares".
- UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1982 ("Ancient City of Sigiriya").
- Moats and ramparts around a planned city; water gardens on the west on an east–west axis with underground conduits; the fountains still run after heavy rain.
- Lion gate of brick and plaster on the north terrace; the stair passed between the paws; only the paws survive. Script says "half way up the rock": **check** the height of the terrace.
- Summit: brick foundations of the palace and a large rock-cut pond.
- Rock type (granite or the plug of an old volcano): sources disagree, so the script does not say.

## Before Kashyapa (H)
- Rock shelters at the foot with drip ledges and Brahmi donor inscriptions, 3rd century BCE to 1st century CE: Buddhist monks lived here long before the king.
- Pidurangala, the next rock, was a monastery throughout; tradition says the monks moved there when the king took Sigiriya (not used in the script).

## Kashyapa's story (M: Culavamsa chapters 38–39)
- Dhatusena, king at Anuradhapura, built great reservoirs, among them Kala Wewa.
- Two sons: Kashyapa, the elder, by a consort who was not of royal rank; Moggallana, the queen's son and heir.
- Migara, the king's nephew and army commander, was married to the king's daughter. He beat her; the king had Migara's mother (his own sister) burned. Migara then backed Kashyapa. The script only says "a family feud". In Tamil மருமகன் covers both nephew and son-in-law.
- 477: Kashyapa seized the throne and imprisoned his father; Moggallana fled to South India. UNESCO gives the reign as 477–495; other chronologies run a few years earlier (473–491). **check** which dates the channel wants; the script uses 477, 495 and "18 years".
- Migara claimed the old king had hidden treasure for Moggallana. Dhatusena led them to Kala Wewa, bathed, drank and said "this is all my wealth". Kashyapa ordered his death; the chronicle says Migara walled him up alive.
- Kashyapa moved the capital to Sigiriya and built "a palace like a second Alakamanda", living like Kuvera.
- 495: Moggallana came back with an army. Kashyapa came down to fight in the open. His elephant turned aside from marshy ground; the army took it for retreat and broke; he took his own life with his dagger. Other tellings exist (fell on his sword; poisoned), not used.
- Moggallana returned the capital to Anuradhapura and gave Sigiriya to the monks.

## How far to trust the chronicle (O)
- This part of the Culavamsa is credited to the monk Dhammakitti, 13th century: about seven centuries after the events, from the side of the Mahavihara monks, hostile to a man it calls a father-killer. No account from Kashyapa's side survives.
- Whether the rock was a refuge built in fear or a statement of god-like kingship: scholars argue both.

## The paintings (H, identity O)
- In a sheltered pocket on the west face about 100 m up. Survivors counted as 18, 19, 21 or 22: script says "about twenty".
- "Five hundred" comes from a verse scratched on the mirror wall centuries later. **check** the verse.
- No inscription names the women. Readings: apsaras; women of the court; Paranavitana's cloud and lightning maidens; Raja de Silva's reading as the goddess Tara (**check**, left out of the script).
- Night of 13 October 1967: paint thrown on many, two hacked away; most restored by the Italian conservator Luciano Maranzi; no one was caught. Script says only "1967", "paint", "most were saved, some were lost".

## The mirror wall (H)
- Brick with highly polished lime plaster, below the paintings.
- Scratched texts: 1,500 to over 1,800 by different counts (script: "over 1,800", **check**), mostly 8th to 10th century, by monks, soldiers, officials and women who signed with name and village.
- Senarat Paranavitana, *Sigiri Graffiti* (1956): 685 verses. Among the oldest Sinhala poetry.
- The closing line of the film: a visitor named Budal wrote, in effect, "I am Budal. I came to see Sigiriya. Since many others wrote, I did not." **check**: quoted from memory of the commonly cited translation; the search extract confirms only that a visitor wrote that he came and did not write because so many had. Confirm the name and wording against Paranavitana.

## After Kashyapa (H)
- A monastery until the 13th or 14th century (script: "about 800 years"); later a Kandyan outpost; then forest.
- 1831: Major Jonathan Forbes recorded it. Local people had always known it.
- H.C.P. Bell, Archaeological Commissioner, worked here from the mid 1890s; the lion paws were uncovered in 1898 (**check** the year).
- Cultural Triangle project from 1982.

## Ravana and other claims (L, C)
- Widely held belief in Sri Lanka: Sigiriya was Ravana's palace, or Kubera's Alakamanda; the summit was a landing place for the Pushpaka vimana. No archaeological evidence; Sri Lanka's official Ramayana trail leaves Sigiriya out. The script calls these belief and claim and says so respectfully.

## Palace or monastery (O)
- Raja de Silva, former Commissioner of Archaeology (**check** the title), *Sigiriya and Its Significance* (2002): never a palace, a Buddhist monastery from start to finish. A minority view.
- Mainstream (Bandaranayake and others): monks before, a royal phase of about 18 years with one burst of building in the late 5th century, monks again after.

## Faces
- No likeness of Kashyapa or his family is known. Every face in the film is invented; the last chapter says so.
