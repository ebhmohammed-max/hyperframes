**12:00 comparison film. Separate from the 35-minute episode; not a replacement or a condensed cut.**

## Recommendation for the full episode

Use **cinematic history** as the connective language, because it carries the scale of the
subject without pretending the audience is watching evidence. Borrow from other
treatments where they genuinely serve the story:

- **Archival and investigative** treatment only for the disputed origin material, where a
  claim, a tradition and an uncertainty must be visibly separated.
- **Maps** only when space is the actual question; treat them as schematics and say so.
- **Anatomy and network diagrams** only when a relationship needs to be explained, and
  always with the "illustrative" label intact.
- **Human-cost pacing** in the mining chapter, with longer holds and less music.

The risk to avoid is a film that is beautiful but rhetorically vague — images that look
like evidence while the narration carries all the argument. The archival and investigative
cards are the safeguard: they keep the sources of each claim visible.

## What each treatment does well / risks

| Treatment | Strength | Risk if overused |
|---|---|---|
| Cinematic | Emotional scale, coherence, watchable openings | Spectacle can imply authenticity |
| Archival | Makes method visible; slows the argument down | Can feel static over 35 minutes |
| Investigative | Honest about open questions | Repeated "unresolved" beats can stall momentum |
| Geographic | Makes space legible | A schematic can be mistaken for a real map |
| Natural history | Explains rather than merely displays | Simplified anatomy can look authoritative |
| Political | Shows structure, not just individuals | A six-node graph can imply a complete dataset |
| Human cost | Restores the moral centre | Can drift into suffering-as-spectacle |
| Reflective essay | Gives the film a thesis and an ending | Abstraction can outrun the evidence |

## What this sampler does not demonstrate

Real interviews or expert testimony; live-action field production; authentic archival
footage; new lore or book fact-checking; measured statistical charts; photoreal 3D or
generated moving dragon footage; an avatar presenter; word-aligned multilingual
subtitles; platform monetization approval; full image or franchise publication clearance.
These are separate workflows requiring source material, permissions and review. None has
been quietly substituted into this video.

## Publication cautions

All illustrations are AI-generated. Narration is synthetic (voice-01). Reading cards are
designed for this sampler and are **not** scans or authenticated manuscripts. Maps are
schematic, without scale. The political network is illustrative, not a genealogy.
Uncertainty labels do not independently validate the underlying lore. Full attribution
is in AUDIO_CREDITS.txt.
